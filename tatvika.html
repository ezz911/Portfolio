Overview

The challenge

Tatvika's existing website had a clear problem — users were landing on the site but not staying. The information hierarchy was unclear, the visual design felt dated, and the user journey had too many dead ends.

The brief was a full redesign: start from user research, understand why users were dropping off, and design a new experience that would keep them engaged and moving through the site.

📉
The Problem
Poor information hierarchy, unclear navigation, and a visual design that didn't reflect the brand's quality — leading to low engagement and high bounce rates.
📈
The Goal
Redesign the entire website through a research-led process — improving hierarchy, visual design, and user journeys to increase engagement measurably.
Process

How I approached it

1
User Research
Conducted user research to understand what visitors were looking for, where they were getting confused, and what was causing them to leave without taking action.
2
Audit of Existing Site
Performed a full UX audit of the existing website — mapping every page, identifying friction points, weak CTAs, inconsistent components, and broken information flows.
3
Wireframing
Built low-fidelity wireframes to restructure the information architecture — getting the skeleton right before applying any visual design.
4
High-Fidelity Figma Prototypes
Translated wireframes into polished, interactive Figma prototypes — applying an improved visual design system with stronger hierarchy, better spacing, and consistent components.
5
Responsive Design Delivery
Delivered both desktop and mobile designs with a streamlined user journey — ensuring a consistent experience across all devices.

Learnings

What I learned

Starting with research rather than redesigning on instinct was the single biggest factor in the 30% engagement lift — understanding why users left before deciding how to fix it.
Information hierarchy is invisible when it works — users don't notice good IA, they just find what they're looking for. The redesign made navigation feel effortless.
Wireframing before high-fidelity design saves enormous time. Getting stakeholder sign-off on structure first means no major structural changes during the visual design phase.

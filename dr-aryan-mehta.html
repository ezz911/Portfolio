Overview

The challenge

Dr. Aryan Mehta is a cardiologist with 18+ years of experience and 5,000+ procedures to his name. Despite his credentials, patients searching online couldn't find a credible digital presence that reflected his expertise.

Medical websites often fail in one of two ways — they feel either too clinical and cold, or too generic and untrustworthy. The goal was to strike the right balance: authoritative but approachable, and fast to navigate for anxious patients.

💊
The Problem
No digital presence reflecting 18+ years of expertise. Patients couldn't find key information — credentials, services, or how to book — quickly enough.
✅
The Goal
Design a trust-first website where patients find what they need in under 3 seconds — and feel confident enough to book an appointment.
Process

How I approached it

1
Patient Psychology Research
Researched what anxious patients look for when choosing a cardiologist — credentials, success rates, approachability, and ease of booking. These became the design priorities.
2
Information Architecture
Structured navigation around five patient needs: Services, About, Credentials, Patient Resources, and Contact. Every path leads to a booking action within two clicks.
3
Hero & Trust Signals
Designed a hero section with floating stat cards — 5,000+ procedures, 18+ years experience — to establish credibility immediately above the fold.
4
Visual Language
Applied a purple-and-white palette with strong typographic hierarchy — communicating clinical credibility with warmth. Avoided cold blues and sterile layouts.
5
Build on Lovable
Implemented the design directly in Lovable — a no-code tool that allowed rapid deployment while maintaining design fidelity across all screen sizes.

Learnings

What I learned

In medical design, trust is the conversion. Every design decision — colour, typography, spacing — needs to answer the question: "does this make the patient feel safe?"
Floating stat cards above the fold are one of the most effective trust-building patterns for service professionals. Numbers do the work that copy can't.
No-code tools like Lovable enable rapid deployment without sacrificing design quality — knowing when to use them is part of being a well-rounded designer.

Overview

The challenge

A1 Guard is a car Paint Protection Film (PPF) brand that needed to establish a credible digital presence from scratch. The client had no brand identity, no website, and no digital marketing — just a service and a goal to grow.

The challenge was to build everything from zero: a brand that communicates quality and protection, a website that converts visitors into enquiries, and ad campaigns that drive measurable revenue.

🎯
The Problem
No brand identity, no digital presence, no way for potential customers to discover or trust the business online.
✅
The Goal
Build a complete brand system, launch a high-converting website, and run Meta ad campaigns to drive direct business revenue.
Process

How I approached it

1
Discovery & Research
Researched the PPF automotive market, competitor brands, and target customer profiles — car owners who care about their vehicle's appearance and long-term value.
2
Brand Identity System
Designed the full identity — logo, colour palette, typography, and iconography. Focused on communicating protection, precision, and automotive authority.
3
Wireframes & Prototyping
Mapped out the website structure in Figma — wireframes first, then high-fidelity prototypes. Prioritised clear service pages, trust signals, and a strong enquiry CTA.
4
Website Design & Handoff
Delivered polished, responsive designs with a developer-ready Figma handoff — component specs, spacing rules, and asset exports.
5
Meta Ad Campaigns
Ran targeted Meta & Instagram campaigns — ad creative designed using UX principles, strong visual hierarchy, and platform-native storytelling. Tested audiences and optimised for conversions.

Learnings

What I learned

Brand identity and marketing need to work as one system — when the visual language is consistent from logo to ad creative, trust compounds across every touchpoint.
For automotive services, social proof and before/after visuals are the highest-converting content. Customers want to see the product in action before they enquire.
Designing the ad creatives with UX principles — clear hierarchy, single focal point, strong CTA — outperformed generic promotional visuals in every A/B test.
